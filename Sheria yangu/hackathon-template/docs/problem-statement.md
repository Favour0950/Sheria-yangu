# Problem & User — Sheria Yangu

## Problem Statement

Young Nairobi residents aged 18-35 -- boda boda riders in Mathare, market vendors in Gikomba,
students in Embakasi -- cannot exercise their Article 118 and Article 196 constitutional rights
to participate in legislation because bills are published only in legal English on platforms
requiring active search, Parliament and county assemblies have no obligation to notify citizens
when a bill is open for comment, and no tool exists that bridges that gap -- leaving the
constitutional right to public participation practically unreachable for the population it was
designed to serve.

## Target User

| Dimension | Detail |
|---|---|
| Primary user | Mobile-first youth aged 18-25 in peri-urban Nairobi constituencies (Mathare, Kibra, Embakasi) -- boda boda riders, market vendors, small business owners |
| Tech comfort | Comfortable with smartphones, WhatsApp, and social media; not comfortable navigating government websites or reading legal English |
| Language | Kiswahili and English (both taught through the standard school system) |
| Current workflow | Learns about bills, if at all, from informal social-media summaries shared by influencers/CivicEducationKE -- informal, unverified, and reaches only those already following those accounts |

## The Specific Gap

**What's already there:** Mzalendo Trust's Dokeza platform, which lets citizens annotate bills
open for public participation; Kenya Law's online bill repository; Parliament's own social
media channels.

**Why it falls short:** Dokeza is English-only, desktop-oriented, relies on manual expert
annotation (slow, doesn't scale to short participation windows), and has no proactive
notification system. Kenya Law publishes bills as dense legal-English PDFs. None of these
alert a citizen that a specific bill is open for comment this week, or explain what it means
for them personally.

**The gap we fill:** A mobile-first tool that (1) notifies citizens when a bill enters its
public participation window, (2) uses an LLM to translate each clause into plain English and
Kiswahili on the spot, and (3) lets the citizen agree, reject, or auto-compile a memorandum
from the clauses they reject -- collapsing a multi-day, legal-English research task into a
five-minute mobile interaction.

## Why It Matters

The Nairobi City County Finance Act 2023 -- which charged boda boda riders Sh1,000/year and
taxi/app-hailing drivers Sh1,500/year -- was declared unconstitutional by the High Court in
June 2025 (Nyabuto v County Assembly of Nairobi, Petition E040 of 2025) specifically because
the county failed to give citizens meaningful information to respond to before the bill passed.
Thousands of riders paid those fees for two years without knowing the bill existed or that they
had a constitutional right to object. Closing this notification-and-comprehension gap means
citizens don't need a lawyer and a court case to be heard before a law that taxes their
livelihood takes effect -- they need a notification on their phone.
