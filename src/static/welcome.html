The problem

A constitutional right that's practically unreachable

Under Article 118 and Article 196 of Kenya's Constitution, citizens have a right to participate in the laws and fees that affect them. In practice, bills are published only in dense legal English, on platforms you have to already know to search, and neither Parliament nor county assemblies are obligated to tell you when one is open for comment. Since 2021, at least three Kenyan laws have been struck down by courts for exactly this failure — but only after they'd already taken effect, and after they'd already affected thousands of people who never knew they had a right to object in the first place.

Who this is for

Built for people the system wasn't built to reach

Mobile-first residents — boda boda riders, market vendors, small business owners, students — who are comfortable with a phone and WhatsApp, but not with navigating a government website or reading legal English. People who read and speak both Kiswahili and English, and who currently only hear about a bill, if at all, from an informal social-media summary shared after the fact.

Why Nairobi first

This pilot starts with Nairobi County bills because of the scale of who's affected here — and expands from there. Future versions carry the same notification and translation pipeline to bills from any county assembly or Parliament, not just Nairobi's Finance Act.

~5.5M residents (2026 projection — Kenya's next census is 2029)
27.4% of Kenya's national GDP, concentrated in one county (KNBS)

How it works

From an open bill to a logged objection

Every step happens in minutes, not months.

Detect→ Translate→ Review→ Notify→ Vote→ Send
1

Detect

Our scrapers check county and national bill sources for anything newly published or newly open for comment.

2

Translate

Each clause is turned into a short, plain-language summary in English and Swahili by an LLM.

3

Review

A human admin checks each summary against the real bill text before it ever reaches a citizen.

4

Notify

You get an SMS or in-app alert the moment a Nairobi bill opens for comment.

5

Vote

Read each clause's summary and accept or reject it — one clause at a time, not the whole bill.

6

Send

Every clause you rejected is drafted into one formal memorandum, addressed and sent to the clerk legally required to log it.
