Terms & data policy

What we collect

Your phone number is never stored as plain text — only a one-way cryptographic hash of it, keyed with a secret pepper only the server holds. No one, including Sheria Yangu's own team, can reverse that hash back into a phone number.

What "pseudonymous" means here

Your votes and memoranda are linked to a randomly generated token, not to your phone number or identity directly. That token is technically reversible only by whoever controls both the identity table and the server-side pepper — deliberately walled off, minimized, and not touched by ordinary app code (like analytics).

Constituency — self-reported, not verified

The constituency you enter is not checked against the voter roll or any national ID system — Sheria Yangu doesn't have access to that data. County participation analytics reflect what people typed in, not confirmed residency.

What county analytics can see

Only a count of votes per constituency. It never queries your identity, phone number, or token — it reads only the anonymous, point-in-time constituency snapshot taken when you voted.

Sending a memorandum

Sheria Yangu never sends anything to Parliament or a county assembly on your behalf automatically. It drafts a memorandum for you to review and edit, then opens your own email app (or gives you the text and address to paste into your own webmail) so you send it yourself. We only record that you did. Before you can vote on a clause, you must confirm you've read and understood its summary — that confirmation is stored alongside your memorandum, so what you send reflects a clause you actually engaged with, not a blind reaction.

One memorandum per bill

You can send one memorandum per bill — this stops the same objection being submitted multiple times by mistake. You can still redraft and edit before you actually send.

Notifications

SMS, email, and in-app notifications are all opt-in, controlled in Settings. Turning them off stops future notifications; it doesn't delete your past votes or memoranda.

How accurate are the AI summaries?

Clause summaries are drafted by an AI model, but we don't claim 100% accuracy — no summarizer, human or AI, can promise that. What we do guarantee is that no summary reaches you until an admin has read the original clause text side by side with the AI's draft and marked it verified. We track this as a simple number: clauses verified out of clauses scraped for a bill. A bill only opens for public participation once every one of its clauses has cleared that review — you can check a bill's own status page to see this for yourself.

Who reviews before you see it

A human admin, not just the AI, checks every clause summary against the source bill PDF before it's shown to citizens. If a summary looks wrong, the admin edits or rejects it and the AI is asked again — you never see an unreviewed draft.

How long we keep your data

Votes, memoranda, and the read/understood confirmations tied to them are kept for as long as the bill they relate to remains relevant to county or national records — this is a hackathon build, so today that means indefinitely, until we publish a formal retention schedule. You can ask us to delete your account's data at any time (see "Your rights" below).

Third parties we share data with

Clause text is sent to DeepSeek (our AI provider) to generate a draft summary — no phone number, name, or identity ever accompanies that request. SMS messages (OTPs, notifications, feedback) pass through our SMS gateway provider to reach your phone. Neither third party receives your votes, memoranda, or constituency.

Your rights over your data

You can ask us to show you what's linked to your account, correct a mistaken constituency entry, or delete your account and its associated votes/memoranda. Because your phone number is stored only as a one-way hash, we can locate your record by re-hashing the number you give us — we can't look you up by name.

Security measures

Phone numbers are hashed, never stored in plain text. Sign-in uses short-lived OTPs and signed session tokens (JWTs), not passwords. Admin actions require a separate admin credential from citizen sign-in. All traffic to the app is encrypted in transit.

Not legal advice

Sheria Yangu helps you understand and participate in a bill's public participation process — it doesn't replace a lawyer, and sending a memorandum through the app doesn't guarantee any particular outcome from Parliament or a county assembly. For legal advice specific to your situation, consult a licensed advocate.

Changes to this policy

If we materially change what we collect or how we use it, we'll update this page and, where practical, notify you in-app before the change takes effect. Continuing to use Sheria Yangu after a change means you accept the updated policy.

Complaints & contact

If you believe your data has been mishandled, or something on this page doesn't match what the app actually does, use the in-app feedback option or the contact details on our welcome page to reach the team directly.
